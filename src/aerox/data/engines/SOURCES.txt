AEROX ENGINE DATA SOURCES

ICAO Engine Emissions Databank
  File: icao-edb-issue-32.xlsx
  Issue: 32
  Publication date: 2026-03-20
  Downloaded: 2026-08-06
  Official page:
    https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/environment/icao-aircraft-engine-emissions-databank
  Direct download:
    https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/downloads/131424/en
  Host: European Union Aviation Safety Agency on behalf of ICAO
  Scope: Production turbojet and turbofan engines regulated by ICAO Annex 16,
    Volume II, generally engines with rated static thrust above 26.7 kN.
  Important limitation: Absence from this database does not mean an engine
    designation is invalid. Smaller business-jet engines, most turboprops,
    piston engines, and several military designations are outside its scope.
  Redistribution: Preserve the official attribution and verify EASA/ICAO terms
    before redistributing the original workbook outside this repository.

Aircraft subtype mappings
  Only engine identifiers present in generated engines.csv may appear in a
  non-null aircraft subtypes mapping. The aircraft TCDS establishes subtype
  applicability; the ICAO EEDB establishes the query identifier spelling.

Nacelle geometry
  File: nacelles.csv
  Use: nacelle, pylon, and nacelle-base terms of the turbofan drag build-up
    in aerox.models.drag.builder.
  Matching: engine_pattern is a regular expression searched in the aircraft
    record's engine.default designation. The first matching row applies.
  Units: metres. fan_diameter_m is the fan (tip) diameter.
    nacelle_max_diameter_m is the largest nacelle cross-section.
    nacelle_length_m runs from the inlet lip to the fan-nozzle exit for a
    short-duct (separate-flow) nacelle and to the common nozzle exit for a
    long-duct (mixed-flow) nacelle.
  Tiers:
    A-measured-nacelle: maximum diameter and length read from Airbus
      "Aircraft Characteristics - Airport and Maintenance Planning" drawings,
      chapter 2-12-0 (figure numbers in nacelle_source). The drawings are
      marked as approximate dimensions. The Trent XWB values come from the
      A350-900 preliminary issue and are also applied to the Trent XWB-97.
    B-fan-diameter-ratio: published fan diameter; nacelle dimensions are
      derived at run time with D_max = 1.25 D_fan and L = 1.95 D_fan (short
      duct) or 3.1 D_fan (long duct). The ratios come from the A-tier drawings
      (D_max/D_fan 1.21-1.31; L/D_fan 1.8-2.1 short duct, 3.0-3.3 long duct)
      and NASA NTRS 20140001089, Table 1 (D_max/D_fan 1.22-1.23).
    C-estimated-fan-diameter (no row): engines without a published fan
      diameter in this file. The builder estimates D_fan = 0.00827
      sqrt(MTOW / engine count), MTOW in kg, and applies the short-duct
      ratios. 0.00827 is the median over the A- and B-tier turbofan records.
  Fan-diameter sources: EASA engine TCDS where they state the fan diameter
    or the fan size in the series designation; GE and CFM product sheets;
    otherwise secondary compilations (public engine family summaries, and the
    engine data file B of Jenkinson, Simpkin and Rhodes, Civil Jet Aircraft
    Design). Secondary values are marked as such in fan_diameter_source.
  Known limitations:
    - Jenkinson data-file values for small engines may be overall engine
      diameters rather than fan diameters. Rows where its two tables disagree
      (BR710, Tay) are not used and fall back to tier C.
    - The FJ44-1 value is not applied to FJ44-2, -3, or -4 engines.
    - Duct type for the small-engine rows is not documented in the collected
      sources; those rows use the short-duct default.
    - No pylon dimensions are published; pylons are 20 % of the nacelle
      friction and form drag.
